Research

How does the atmosphere move—and why does it vary?

My work follows the regional expression of the Hadley circulation, connecting large-scale climate structure with the disturbances that reshape it from week to week.

Regional Hadley circulations tell distinct stories

Global-mean studies can obscure important regional differences in strength and extent. This work offers a comprehensive view of Hadley circulations across six geographic domains and asks what makes each region behave differently.

Reference: Pratiksha & Neena (2023), AGU Fall Meeting Abstract ↗

Tropical eddies modulate circulation strength

Deep convection triggered by low-level convergence associated with mixed Rossby–gravity waves plays a key role in modulating the strength of the East Pacific Hadley circulation.

Reference: Baruah, Neena & Suhas (2025), JGR: Atmospheres ↗

Extratropical circulation reaches into the tropics

Westerly wind ducts over the eastern Pacific and Atlantic during boreal winter enable potential-vorticity intrusions from the extratropics. I investigate how these events influence regional mass flux as well as circulation strength and extent.
